The service problem

Seasonal fulfillment creates pressure at every handoff: orders must be picked, consolidated, sorted by destination, staged, loaded, dispatched, delivered, and reconciled. When the physical operation and the digital status model drift apart, people spend time reconstructing what happened instead of moving the order forward.

Design response

Organize by territory before the loading bay.

The proposal divides delivery work into nine geographic zones and carries that identity through picking, staging, loading, and dispatch so the physical layout and the information model speak the same language.

Model the full lifecycle.

Thirteen stages connect the customer order, ZIP-based zone assignment, picking, loading, dispatch, delivery, and wave close. A single “ready” status is not enough for a workflow with this many handoffs.

Design the exception path on purpose.

Orders with damaged items, missing items, address problems, payment issues, customer changes, or special instructions move to an exception station with a visible reason and owner instead of disappearing into side conversations.

Proposed operating flow

The interactive proposal follows the original thirteen-step operating model:

01–04 · IDENTIFYCustomer order → ZIP → Zone → Color
05–07 · PREPAREPicker → Rack → Bagger
08–10 · LOADLoader verification → Bay → Driver load
11–13 · COMPLETEDispatch → Delivery → Wave close

Explore all eleven destinations in the demo: the overview, nine-zone map, LOT route maps, bay layout, color reference, volume model, thirteen-step flow, multilingual signage, and three complete planning documents.

What this demonstrates

This project connects my operations background directly to product and service design. The interface is only one part of the system; the stronger design problem is making digital status, physical location, ownership, and the next action agree with one another.

The proposal is structured for validation against actual order volume, staffing, space, and exception patterns before any performance claim is made.
