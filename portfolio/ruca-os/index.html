Independent product project · Desktop · 2026

RUCA OS

Making a powerful PC feel like a coherent product rather than a collection of disconnected utilities.

My contribution
Product direction, information architecture, interaction requirements, visual direction, and implementation review
Working method
Self-directed design with Figma and AI-assisted prototyping
Presentation
Local Windows product work + isolated public browser simulation

The problem

Moving from a console to a PC introduces a different kind of work: choosing between launchers, monitoring utilities, media controls, settings, and information feeds. The product brief asks how that flexibility could feel more coherent without hiding the machine’s state.

I organized the experience around what the person needs to understand and do, rather than around which utility owns each function.

Key decisions

Readiness before a wall of choices.

The System Heart establishes a central point of orientation, while surrounding instruments retain access to machine detail. The tradeoff is less immediate emphasis on shortcuts in exchange for a clearer starting point.

Meaning before measurement.

Diagnostics should explain severity, meaning, and the next check before exposing deeper sensor detail. Missing information must remain unavailable or unknown rather than appearing as a reassuring zero.

Shared interaction rules, distinct destinations.

Navigation, visible focus, selection, and recovery should remain consistent while each destination serves a different task. This is the interaction layer connecting readiness, launching, information, diagnostics, and controls.

My role

I defined the product intent, navigation, information hierarchy, visual direction, and acceptance criteria. I used AI-assisted development to iterate on implementations and reviewed the result against those requirements.

This self-directed project sits alongside my professional background in postal operations and hospitality.

What this demo represents

A documented public snapshot of the interaction design. Its values and external information are demonstration data. It cannot control your device and is not the native Android companion or a live connection to my workstation.

Evidence & limits

The available evidence includes runtime captures, a public interactive simulation, documented interaction requirements, and repeated implementation review. These demonstrate the work and its scope, not a measured improvement in customer outcomes.

Moderated testing with target users is not presented as complete. The next research questions are whether people understand readiness, can find a destination, and interpret an unavailable or degraded state without assistance.
